Planning exterior cleaning for a large Cotswold home

Plan window, gutter and exterior cleaning for a substantial Cotswold property, taking account of access, stonework, seasons and regular maintenance.

19 November 2026

All articles

A larger home often has several different exterior surfaces, rooflines and access requirements. Looking after them as separate jobs can lead to repeated visits, unnecessary disruption and missed areas. A planned approach is particularly useful for older Cotswold properties, where stonework, leaded windows and landscaping need considered treatment.

Walk around the property before setting a schedule

Record the different elevations, extensions, garages, conservatories, rooflights and any detached buildings. Identify where trees overhang the house and where gutters are difficult to inspect from the ground. Note which gates are normally locked and where vehicles can safely park.

For a property with staff or a managing agent, nominate a contact who can agree access and receive reports. Keep photographs of areas of concern rather than asking someone to climb onto a roof to investigate.

Prioritise windows and gutters according to exposure

Windows close to trees, roads or exposed gardens may need a different routine from sheltered glass. High-level glass and conservatory roofs may require different access arrangements. Agree exactly which elevations, frames, sills and doors are included in each visit.

Gutters and outlets also deserve scheduled attention, especially where leaves gather in autumn. A contractor can advise on access and report signs of damage, leaks or recurring obstructions. Cleaning is not a substitute for repairing damaged rainwater goods.

Take care around stone and delicate finishes

Cotswold stone, older mortar, painted joinery and leaded glass all need an approach suited to their condition. Avoid assuming that higher pressure is better. Ask how the contractor will assess the surface and protect planting, drainage, doors and neighbouring areas. Listed properties may require particular care and advice before more invasive work.

Arrange exterior work around the household

Where practical, schedule disruptive patio or driveway work outside important events and specify areas that should remain accessible. Agree how hard-to-reach work will be undertaken by trained professionals. Allow for weather-related changes rather than insisting that work at height proceeds in unsafe conditions.

Build a maintenance plan that can change

Agree a regular window cleaning frequency, seasonal gutter inspections and review dates for other exterior surfaces. Keep notes after each visit, including anything that needs a specialist repair. Different properties need different intervals, so adjust the plan to what is observed over time.
